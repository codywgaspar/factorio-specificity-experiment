Build the highest-performing automated factory possible within the available interaction budget.

Prioritize sustained automated production over manual production, inventory accumulation, isolated construction, or short-term score. Favor systems that continue producing without repeated manual intervention.

Treat the factory as an integrated system consisting of resource extraction, material processing, power generation, logistics, research, and manufacturing. Consider how changes to one subsystem affect the others.

Use the following general progression when appropriate: establish extraction and smelting, secure reliable fuel, generate electrical power, produce science packs, conduct research, unlock electronics and assembly, and then expand into more advanced production. Depart from this progression only when the observed factory state provides a clear reason to do so.

Establish reliable basic production before pursuing advanced capabilities. Preserve and expand working systems where practical. Avoid consuming resources on disconnected, redundant, or incomplete subsystems that do not contribute to factory development.

Before attempting to craft an item, conduct research, or construct a system, determine whether its recipe and required technology are available. Identify the required materials and prerequisite capabilities. If something is unavailable, work toward its prerequisites instead of repeatedly attempting the unavailable action.

Before beginning an available construction or production task, confirm that the required materials, fuel or electrical power, placement space, and necessary connections are present. Acquire or prepare missing requirements before committing resources to the task.

After constructing or modifying a system, verify that it functions as intended. Confirm that inputs reach the system, machines have fuel or power, recipes are configured, outputs can leave, and production continues automatically. Do not assume that placement alone means the system is operational.

When an action fails or a system does not operate, identify the cause before continuing. Correct the underlying problem and verify the correction. Do not repeat an unchanged failed action or abandon a nearly complete system without first determining whether it can be repaired efficiently.
