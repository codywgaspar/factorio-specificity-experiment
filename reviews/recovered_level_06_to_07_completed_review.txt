ADAPTIVE REVIEW: RECOVERED LEVEL 6 TO LEVEL 7

What improved:
The simplified Level 6 instruction produced a strong recovery. Mean production
increased approximately 37%, automated production increased approximately 52%,
and average errors decreased approximately 43%. Placement errors decreased from
57 to 20, while productive turns increased from 118.3 to 128.7.

What still went wrong:
The model did not always stop after two failures. Two runs included placement
loops of nine and eleven consecutive turns. In both cases, the model attempted
to place a wooden chest that was not present in inventory and changed
coordinates repeatedly before checking the actual prerequisite.

Strategic limitation:
All three runs remained focused on basic iron, coal, stone, furnaces, and burner
equipment. None of the runs issued a research command. Performance improved,
but the factory did not consistently progress toward copper processing, power,
research, logistics, or automated manufacturing.

Options considered:
1. Strengthen the priority of the two-failure stopping rule.
2. Require a diagnostic check after the first failure.
3. Shift the next intervention toward long-term factory strategy.
4. Combine another error-control rule with a strategic objective.

Decision:
The team selected the long-term strategy option. Level 6 recovered enough
performance that continuing to refine placement behavior could overemphasize
local errors while the factory remains strategically basic. The existing
failure rule remains active as part of the cumulative prompt.

Level 7 intervention:
Once basic production is functioning, the model should select one achievable
factory-development objective and carry it forward across turns. The model
should preserve functioning production while avoiding unnecessary maintenance
and repeated manual gathering.
