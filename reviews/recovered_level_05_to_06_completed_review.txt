ADAPTIVE REVIEW: RECOVERED LEVEL 5 TO LEVEL 6

What improved:
Level 5 reduced inspection activity by approximately 11%, addressing the
previous inspection-loop bottleneck. The model acted more often and showed
better follow-through between its stated plan and executable action.

What became worse:
Placement errors increased from 3 at Level 4 to 57 at Level 5. Mean production
fell by approximately 29%, and mean automated production fell by approximately
39%. The model frequently repeated failed placements instead of changing
objectives.

Interpretation:
The Level 5 instruction successfully encouraged action, but it did not provide
a sufficiently clear stopping rule. In several runs, passive inspection loops
were replaced by aggressive placement-failure loops.

Options considered:
1. Add a fixed placement cooldown.
2. Require a material state change before retrying.
3. Force a specific productive fallback after two failures.
4. Simplify the instruction to stop retrying any action after two failures.

Decision:
The team selected the simplified stopping rule. This keeps the intervention
general, reduces prompt complexity, and allows us to test whether a concise
instruction prevents repeated failures without limiting productive autonomy.

Level 6 intervention:
If an action fails twice, stop retrying it and switch to productive work.
Changing coordinates, quantities, or minor details does not make it a new
action. The failed objective may be resumed only after the inventory or game
state has meaningfully changed.
