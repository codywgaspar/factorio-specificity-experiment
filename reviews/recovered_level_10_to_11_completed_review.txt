ADAPTIVE REVIEW: RECOVERED LEVEL 10 TO LEVEL 11

What improved:
- All three runs successfully produced copper plates.
- The first copper plates appeared on Turns 29, 52, and 30.
- Mean production increased from 3,829 to 4,919, a 28% improvement.
- Mean automated production increased from 1,245 to 1,512, a 21% improvement.
- Mean errors decreased from 34.0 to 9.7, a 72% reduction.
- The longest error streak decreased from 9.3 to 2.0 turns.
- Error recovery increased to 100%.
- Production variability decreased from a 32.0% coefficient of variation
  to 8.4%.
- Automated-production variability decreased from 39.5% to 11.6%.
- No placement errors occurred.

What did not improve:
- The model continued pursuing copper production after the completion condition
  had been met.
- No power system, science production, active research program, or advanced
  manufacturing chain followed.
- Manual harvesting increased by 33%.
- Inspection turns increased by 11%.
- Automated production per 1,000 ticks decreased by 4%.
- The longest repeated-action streak increased from 8.7 to 10.0 turns.
- One run repeated copper-furnace loading for 14 consecutive turns.

Interpretation:
Assigning one concrete capability was highly effective. It increased production,
reduced errors, lowered between-run variation, and produced the required new
capability in every run. However, the model became fixated on the completed
objective and did not transition to the next stage of factory development.

Options considered:
1. Assign steam power as the next concrete capability.
2. Add only a general completion-transition rule.
3. Automate copper extraction before pursuing another subsystem.
4. Provide a longer roadmap covering power, research, and manufacturing.

Decision:
Option 1 was selected. Level 11 retains copper-plate production as the first
milestone because each run begins from an empty factory. Once at least one
copper plate is produced, the model must shift immediately to establishing
functioning steam power.

Reason for the decision:
The Level 10 results show that the model responds well to a concrete,
measurable objective. Steam power is the next logical factory capability and
is a prerequisite for powered research and manufacturing. The sequential
instruction also tests whether the model can recognize completion and change
objectives during a run.

Level 11 success indicators:
- Copper plates are produced.
- The model transitions away from copper after satisfying that milestone.
- An offshore pump, boiler, and steam engine are obtained and connected.
- At least one steam engine actively produces electricity.
- Iron and copper expansion does not replace the steam-power objective.
- Failed actions are not repeated more than twice without a meaningful change.
- Placement and API errors remain controlled.
- The model selects another unmet capability if steam power is completed early.
